
# Soap Membrane Formation and Expansion Tests

## 01. Objective

The purpose of this experiment was to investigate the formation, stability, and expansion of soap membranes using circular 3D-printed structures.

These preliminary tests explored whether a soap membrane could be formed between two rings and subsequently stretched through controlled separation.

The experiments also investigated the response of the soap membrane to airflow, providing insight into potential methods of generating and manipulating bubbles within RESPIRA.

## 02. Materials and Equipment

- 3D-printed circular rings
- 3D-printed ring holder
- Dishwashing liquid
- Water
- Container for soap solution
- Manual lifting mechanism
- Camera for photographic documentation

## 03. Experimental Setup

A series of circular rings and a supporting holder were fabricated using 3D printing.

A soap solution was prepared by mixing dishwashing liquid with water.

The circular components were brought into contact with the solution to create a thin soap film across the opening.

The upper ring was then manually lifted away from the lower ring to investigate whether the film could stretch between the two circular boundaries without immediately rupturing.

The separation distance was varied manually to observe changes in membrane geometry and stability.

## 04. Experiment A: Soap Membrane Formation and Expansion

### Procedure

1. Prepare a mixture of dishwashing liquid and water.
2. Position the 3D-printed rings within the supporting holder.
3. Bring the rings into contact with the soap solution.
4. Slowly lift the upper ring away from the lower ring.
5. Observe the formation and stretching of the soap membrane.
6. Continue separating the rings to investigate how the membrane geometry changes.
7. Document the membrane at different stages of expansion.

### Observations

The experiments demonstrated that a soap membrane could form between the two circular rings and stretch as the distance between them increased.

As the upper ring was lifted, the membrane developed a continuous curved surface connecting the two circular boundaries.

The shape changed progressively with increasing separation, producing a narrowed central region.

These observations demonstrated the potential for using the separation of circular frames to generate a visible, three-dimensional soap membrane.

## 05. Experiment B: Airflow Interaction

### Objective

A secondary experiment investigated how a soap membrane supported by the circular structure responded to airflow.

This test explored the possibility of manipulating the membrane through direct air pressure rather than solely through mechanical movement.

### Procedure

1. Form a soap membrane across the circular structure.
2. Direct airflow towards or through the membrane by blowing.
3. Observe the resulting deformation or bubble formation.
4. Document the interaction photographically.

### Observations

The experiment explored the relationship between applied airflow and the behaviour of the soap membrane.

Airflow was used to investigate whether the film could deform or form bubbles while supported by the printed structure.

The experiment provided an additional reference for understanding how air pressure influences soap film geometry.

## 06. Membrane Stability and Limitations

The preliminary experiments were qualitative and conducted through manual manipulation of the rings.

Several factors were identified as relevant to future testing:

- **Separation speed:** The rate at which the rings move may influence the stability of the membrane.
- **Separation distance:** Increasing the distance between the rings changes the geometry of the soap film.
- **Soap concentration:** The composition of the soap solution may affect membrane formation and stability.
- **Surface properties:** The material and surface finish of the rings may influence wetting and film adhesion.
- **Environmental conditions:** Airflow, temperature, and humidity may influence the lifetime of the membrane.
- **Repeatability:** Manual movement does not provide precise control over speed or separation distance.

These factors were not systematically quantified during the preliminary experiments.

### Membrane Failure

If a membrane ruptures during testing, the conditions and approximate stage of expansion should be documented.

## 07. Findings and Design Implications

The preliminary tests demonstrated that a continuous soap membrane could be formed and stretched between two circular frames.

This provided an initial physical proof of concept for the RESPIRA installation.

The experiments informed the development of a mechanical system capable of moving the rings relative to one another.

In particular, they highlighted the importance of controlling the movement of the frames to achieve gradual membrane expansion and contraction.

The airflow experiments also provided an opportunity to compare two different approaches to manipulating soap films: mechanical displacement and applied airflow.

## 08. Further Development

Further experiments could investigate:

1. The maximum separation distance before membrane rupture.
2. The relationship between movement speed and membrane stability.
3. The influence of soap concentration on film lifetime.
4. The repeatability of membrane formation over multiple cycles.
5. The behaviour of the membrane during motor-controlled expansion and contraction.

These investigations would help establish more reliable operating parameters for the final installation.
